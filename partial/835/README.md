# Erdős Problem #835 — partial results

**Problem** ([erdosproblems.com/835](https://www.erdosproblems.com/835)). Is there k > 2 such that the k-element subsets of {1,…,2k} can be coloured with k+1 colours so that inside every (k+1)-element subset, its k+1 subsets of size k all get different colours? (Equivalently: is the chromatic number of the Johnson graph J(2k,k) equal to k+1?) It works for k = 2. **Prize:** none listed.
**Status of the problem** (as of 2026-11-25): open, marked verifiable (one example settles it). Erdős–Rosenfeld posed it. Ma–Tang: impossible for every k > 2 with k+1 not prime. Known in the literature and forum: impossible for all k ≤ 15 (constant-weight code bounds for 3 ≤ k ≤ 14; k = 10 and 12 also via nonexistence of S(4,5,15) and S(4,5,17)); each colour class equals its own set of complements. First open case: k = 16 (17 Steiner systems S(15,16,32) partitioning the 16-subsets of 32 points), which would need an S(4,5,21), itself open. Symmetry searches by others exclude AGL(5,2) and PGL(2,31); an element of prime order p with k+1 < p ≤ 2k cannot preserve a colouring.

Nothing below resolves any case k ≥ 16. All nonexistence facts for k ≤ 15 are known results, not ours.

## Results here
1. **[Reduction]** *Known equivalences, re-derived.* A valid colouring is a partition of the k-subsets into k+1 disjoint Steiner systems S(k−1, k, 2k), and #835 at k holds exactly when a large set LS(k−2, k−1, 2k−1) exists (colour the (k−1)-subsets of 2k−1 points with k+1 colours so that the sets through any fixed (k−2)-set get distinct colours). Both directions were proved in the session; the write-up is not preserved, and the equivalence also appears in Ma–Tang's context and a public AI report. The divisibility conditions for S(k−1,k,2k) were found to allow exactly the k that Ma–Tang leave open (k+1 prime), and the block-intersection test does not rule out S(9,10,20).
2. **[Computed]** *k = 10 refuted by a purely local window check.* Fix a block and its complement; for every one of the 396 isomorphism types of "star" (1-factorization of K10 seen from one point) the triple-window SAT instance is unsatisfiable. A new local proof that S(9,10,20) does not exist (a known fact). Minimal cores (two minimized): about 30 of 240 rule groups, namely all 8 outside-triple rules through one pair of outside points plus about 22 inside rules. Caveat: SAT solver proof files were not kept, so this rests on solver correctness.
3. **[Proved]** *Holonomy facts.* From one block U and its complement W, each pair uv ⊂ U gives a fixed-point-free involution σ_uv of W, and at each point these form a 1-factorization of W. (a) Fixed points of the triangle holonomy σ_uv σ_vw σ_wu correspond exactly to Pasch configurations through uvw in the derived triple system (short proof in the session, not preserved). (b) If every holonomy is trivial ("flat"), the involutions commute and generate a regular elementary abelian 2-group on W, so W ≅ F_2^n and k = |W| is a power of 2; flatness is impossible at k = 12, and among open cases it can occur only when k is a power of 2 with k+1 prime (k = 16, 256, 65536 for the known Fermat primes). Measured: S(3,4,8) flat; S(5,6,12) fixed-point-free involutive holonomy; both STS(13) and the cyclic STS(19) curved.
4. **[Computed]** *Rigidity conjecture refuted.* The conjecture "the corner rules force flat holonomy" is false: a verified valid fill of the 32-point corner (k = 16) is non-flat (2 of 14 outside triples and 5 of 25 inside triangles fix only 12 of 16 points). Corner runs at k = 16: affine (Z₂⁴) star, 22 fillable (all flat) and 33 undecided; GK₁₆ star, 55 of 55 undecided; a translation-invariant flat triangle layer is unsatisfiable.
5. **[Proved]** *Spectral bounds are exactly tight (fake world A).* The uniform fractional large set (weight 1/(k+1) on every set) satisfies every linear and counting condition. For the Johnson graph plus disjointness edges, by hand λ_min = −(t+2) and the eigenvalue bound equals exactly 1/(t+3) for every even t; numerically tight at t = 2 and 4, where the answer is no. So no Hoffman/Delsarte-type bound can exclude any case.
6. **[Computed]** *Integral overlap counts are feasible where the answer is no (fake world B).* Whole, non-negative counts of how a third block meets two given blocks exist at k = 10 and 12; cross-colour three-block counts (with the colours forced to partition every k-set, self-complementary classes, forced zeros) are integrally feasible at k = 4 and 6 (exact rational elimination plus CP-SAT).
7. **[Computed]** *Sherali–Adams certificates.* Degree 4 refutes k = 4 (631 orbit variables), while degree-4 controls with known solutions (k = 2, K6 1-factorizations, large sets of STS(9)) stay feasible; degree 4 (levels 4–6) and degree 5 (level 4; 79k variables, 2.6M inequalities) are blind at k = 6. Degree-2 SOS and degree-3 SA are blind on every impossible case tried.
8. **[Heuristic]** *Tile-twist sign law (triples).* For large sets of triple systems, glue the per-point 1-factorizations ("tiles") along shared triples; a large set exists iff every triangle has trivial holonomy. Observed law: holonomy parity = −(product of three per-tile "star-signs"). Checked exhaustively at 7 points (279,936 tile choices × 35 triangles), on all 84 triangles of a real large set of STS(9), and on ~10,000 random samples at 9–17 points; not proved.
9. **[Reduction]** *Item 8 implies no large set of STS(7) (Cayley 1850, reproved), with no case search beyond the K6 tiles.* The law turns into a tournament on the points. All 6 labelled 1-factorizations of K6 have constant star-sign (exhaustive), which forces every point to beat everyone or lose to everyone, impossible. At 9 points 5,790 of the 6,240 tiles are non-constant, so 9 is not wrongly excluded. Novelty of this sign/tournament argument unchecked.
10. **[Proved]** *Symmetry-matching lemma.* If a colouring has a symmetry g (a point permutation mapping colour classes to colour classes via a colour permutation π), then for every (k+1)-set Y with g(Y) = Y, g restricted to Y has the same cycle type as π: the map y ↦ colour of Y∖{y} is a bijection intertwining g on Y with π. Checked against all 23 non-identity symmetries of the k = 2 solution. The forum's prime-order result is a special case. Novelty beyond that case unchecked.
11. **[Computed]** *Classification of possible symmetries at k = 16* (item 10 for every power g^j, plus "π fixes a colour if g fixes a 16-set" and the forum's adjacency rule, over all cycle types): 176 (point type, colour type) pairs survive; element orders only 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17 and products (none of order 19, 23, 29, 31); the only kind cycling all 17 colours is a 17-cycle fixing 15 points; among elements normalizing it, only the reflection x ↦ −x (one fixed point and 7 swaps on the other 15) survives, so the group generated that way is at most dihedral of order 34 (larger groups containing the rotation otherwise not excluded). A search under that group still faces about 18 million orbits of 16-sets.

## Routes tried and why they failed
- Divisibility, overlap counts, eigenvalue and LP bounds — fail on fake world A at every size (item 5).
- Windows, corners, local SAT — worked at k = 10 but undecided at k = 16 (item 4); too many stars to enumerate.
- Point-by-point reduction — settles k < 16, then needs S(4,5,21), which is open.
- Tile-twist tool, signs only — SAT calibration: kills 7 and 8 points, allows 9 (correct), but allows 10 and 11 points (#835 at k = 6), where the answer is no; 8-point tiles carry 44 star-sign patterns (mod flip), enough freedom. Gluing closed: for ≥ 5 colours the only abelian summary of a holonomy permutation is its sign (A_n is the only proper nontrivial normal subgroup of S_n), and loops of three tiles inside one 4-set cancel identically.
- Chirality, Latin-square parity (identically 1), binary gluing — no information or kills only the fake.
- Integer lattices mod k+1 — reduce to signed designs, which always exist under divisibility (Graver–Jurkat, Wilson).
- Graph homomorphisms/cores — the best theorems stop at exactly "core is complete or not", which is #835.
- Hypercontractivity — colour classes exceed average spikiness by ~(k+1)^{1/4}, allowed up to ~3^{k/2}.
- Perfect 2-colourings of J(2k,k) — merging colours loses the interlocking; at k = 4 every merged 2-colouring exists.
- Folding the middle-levels graph (15/16-subsets of 31 points) onto K17 — equivalent (perfect codes in the doubled Johnson graph); Lloyd's test passes.
- Hypercube toy model — "linear" colourings fail already at k = 2 by pigeonhole; the slice has no regular abelian group.
- Kempe chains (single component for every pair tested), tensor-network signs (incoherent across real solutions), coset enumerators (harder than the problem), topology (Borsuk–Ulam spaces too thin).
- Arithmetic method of the 2026 Delsarte-conjecture preprints (Etzion–Zhang–Zhong; Zhang–Zhong) — does not transfer: at prime k+1 every divisibility condition passes.
